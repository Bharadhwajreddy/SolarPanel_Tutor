Multi-carrier delivery hub

One shop in a Hyderabad suburb that delivers parcels for several logistics companies, and also sells courier bookings to local people and sellers. This page explains how it works, what each step looks like, and what the numbers say.

Short answer

It can work, but slowly, and only under clear conditions. Delivering parcels for companies leaves you only about ₹3.50 a parcel. The real profit comes from the counter: walk-in courier bookings and shipping for local sellers. In the base case you need about ₹11.5 lakh of cash at the lowest point (most of it is deposits and money the companies still owe you), the business becomes profitable every month from month 19, and it pays back in about 4 years.

Working with many companies does not make each parcel more profitable. Each company wants its own riders, app logins and cash ledger, so routes don't get denser. What it does is spread rent and staff over more parcels, and protect you if one company leaves. Valmo's public delivery agreement has no clause stopping you from working for others, but it can end the deal on 3 working days' notice and change fees at any time. Read every contract before signing.

Tax, with one invoice

Delivery work is a courier service (SAC 9968) taxed at 18% GST. Register from day one. Companies deduct TDS of 1% if you are a proprietor (2% for a firm). Example: 4,000 parcels × ₹19 = ₹76,000, plus ₹13,680 GST = ₹89,680 invoice. The company deducts about ₹760 TDS and transfers about ₹88,900. The TDS comes back as an income-tax credit. The GST is often due before the company pays you, which is one reason you need working capital.

Last-mile delivery is mostly pass-through: riders take ₹14.50 of every ₹19. That matches the big companies' own filings. Shadowfax, with ₹1,806 crore half-year revenue, keeps only a 3.56% EBITDA margin. So use delivery work to bring parcels and footfall, and make your profit at the counter.

Delivery payouts (₹14–25 a parcel) come from unofficial sources; no company publishes a rate card. Rider cost ₹12–17 comes from Hyderabad job listings. Counter prices ₹60–100 come from courier blogs; Shiprocket costs are from its official pricing page.

4 · Demand

There are enough parcels. The limit is how many PIN codes you are given.

India shipped 4.9–5.3 billion e-commerce parcels in FY25 (about 14 million a day), growing 23–27% a year. No source gives Hyderabad's share; at an assumed 3–4%, the city moves about 0.5–0.7 million parcels a day. A hub doing 480 a day is under 0.1% of that.

9 · Risks

Businesses like this fail when a client leaves or pays late

Ecom Express let one client (almost certainly Meesho) reach 52% of revenue. Meesho moved its parcels to its own network, Valmo; Ecom closed about 1,000 delivery centres and was sold cheaply to Delhivery. Dunzo left suppliers unpaid, including about ₹4 crore owed to the firm that recruited its riders. Even Shadowfax gets 48.9% of its revenue from Flipkart.

10 · Decision

Go ahead only if all five are true

  1. A written rate of ₹19 or more per delivered parcel, ideally ₹20–21, with rider pay at ₹14.50 or less.At ₹17 the base case does not pay back in 5 years. At ₹21 it pays back in about 35 months.
  2. Two companies from different groups live before the first festive season. No single company above 40% after month 12.Ecom Express failed at 52% from one client.
  3. Counter, seller shipping and packing giving at least half your profit by month 24.Delivery work alone cannot cover rent and staff. In the base case these give 66%.
  4. Access to about ₹11–12 lakh in three parts (₹5 lakh to start), or about ₹4 lakh if optimistic rates are confirmed in writing.Most of it is deposits and invoices waiting to be paid.
  5. You run the floor yourself in year 1, and you stop at the month-5 check if the numbers say stop.Saves about ₹3.3 lakh of cash and 13 months of payback, and limits the loss to about ₹3 lakh.

If you can't meet conditions 1 and 4, do the smaller version: a counter with aggregator bookings and Amazon Hub Delivery from a small shop, with no delivery hub.
