# Thin per-parcel spreads sink three-rider hubs

As designed (a rented hub, three hired riders and a paid route optimizer as the way to make money), the idea does not work at the payout rates the evidence supports. The core problem is the spread. No network publishes a hub rate card. The most-repeated unofficial figure is **₹18-25 per delivered parcel** ([Franchify](https://priteetraffictail.wixsite.com/franchify/post/valmo-delivery-partner-earnings-profit)), and one Valmo-styled calculator uses **₹14** ([valmalogistic.in](https://valmalogistic.in/)). Hyderabad riders are hired at **₹12-17 per parcel**, for example "16+1 per packet" on a Meesho listing in KPHB ([WorkIndia](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/delivery_boy-kphb-hyderabad-5986942/)). Once riders are paid, the hub keeps roughly ₹2-8 per parcel to cover about ₹18,000 a month of rent and overheads. My model gives three results. At a base case of **135 delivered parcels a day and ₹18 a parcel, the hub loses about ₹14,000 a month** and only breaks even at about ₹22 a parcel. The optimistic case (210 parcels a day at ₹22) clears about **₹16,000 a month**, a payback of roughly 12-19 months on ₹3 lakh. The pessimistic case (75 a day at ₹14) uses up the capital in about four months. Three riders are enough capacity to reach break-even. They are not enough to make the business worth owning unless the owner rides too. A route optimizer barely helps: the one large Indian study finds riders spend about **8.8 minutes per parcel**, mostly at the doorstep, with **43% of parcels under 500 m apart** ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). My recommendation is **no-go on the plan as stated**. The alternative is a near-zero-capital pilot that only becomes a hub after a partner commits rates and PIN-code volume in writing.

## No network publishes a hub rate, and filings cap it near ₹30

Every rupee-per-parcel figure for hub partners comes from franchise-marketing sites, not from the networks. Valmo's official app describes per-order fees plus performance incentives but gives no rupee figure ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.valmo.valmo&hl=en_IN)). XpressBees' partner pages are equally silent ([XpressBees](https://www.xpressbees.com/partner/franchise-partner)). The figures in circulation all come from third parties: ₹18-25 for Shadowfax ([franchisehurt](https://franchisehurt.com/shadowfax-franchise-india/)), ₹10-50 "by service type" for Ekart ([nextwhatbusiness](https://nextwhatbusiness.com/ekart-franchise-cost-in-india/)), and a pickup fee of ₹3-8 plus a delivery fee of ₹8-20 for Ekart hubs ([FRANticc](https://franticc.com/brands/ekart-logistics)). Amazon Hub Delivery is the only programme with an official structure: a "guaranteed fixed amount per delivery", paid monthly. Amazon does not publish that amount ([About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/i-have-space-discover-an-extra-source-of-income-with-zero-investments)).

Company filings set a hard ceiling on what any hub can be paid. Shadowfax earns about **₹47-50 per order**, and partner payouts were **63% of express revenue in FY25** ([Mann Ashar, from DRHP](https://mannashar.substack.com/p/shadowfax-tech)). That is roughly ₹30 per order for *every* partner in the chain (first mile, line haul and last mile), so a last-mile hub's share realistically sits in the teens. Valmo's whole-shipment cost fell from **₹55.6 to about ₹41** per order between FY23 and FY26 ([Business Standard broker report](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2026-01/17679504020.34145800.pdf)). JM Financial projects ₹37-38 by FY30 ([summary](https://tvsweekly.substack.com/p/inside-meeshos-2300-cr-turnaround)). The parent networks themselves earn almost nothing per parcel. Shadowfax's profit is about **15 paise per order**, and its spending on lost and damaged shipments was about **7 times its net profit** ([Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/can-shadowfax-deliver)). Payout rates are under steady downward pressure, and loss costs get pushed down the chain.

Marketing earnings claims fall apart against their own arithmetic. The widely repeated "₹30,000-50,000 a month" for a Valmo hub is **gross** revenue at about 80 parcels a day. The same source puts net at ₹12,000-22,000, and one blog's own margin table gives just **₹3,400-7,800 a month at 50 parcels a day with hired riders** ([Franchify](https://priteetraffictail.wixsite.com/franchify/post/valmo-delivery-partner-earnings-profit)). The "₹35,000-70,000 net" claim needs **400-800 parcels a day** ([valmalogistic.in](https://valmalogistic.in/)), which is far more than three riders can carry. The only first-hand owner account found describes a Valmo hub receiving **20-25 shipments a day** against fixed rent, which then closed ([Medium](https://medium.com/@swapnashupradhan5492/the-harsh-reality-behind-my-valmo-franchise-success-story-what-they-dont-tell-you-4ad1a5c93caf)). No network guarantees a minimum volume.

Amazon pays on the first working day of the following month ([About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/i-have-space-discover-an-extra-source-of-income-with-zero-investments)), and XpressBees reportedly pays monthly as well ([juststartup](https://juststartup.in/blogs/xpressbees-franchise-india-apply-cost-courier-delivery-2026)). The owner therefore pays 30-45 days of riders, rent and fuel before the first payment arrives. Rent is the anchor cost. A 300 sq ft unit in Alwal or Bolarum lists at **₹6,000-10,000 with a one-month deposit**, a 520 sq ft unit on Kompally Road at **₹18,000 with a ₹36,000 deposit**, and prime units run ₹25,000-50,000 ([NoBroker Kompally](https://www.nobroker.in/rent-commercial-shops-in-kompally_hyderabad); [99acres LB Nagar](https://www.99acres.com/commercial-shops-for-rent-in-lb-nagar-hyderabad-ffid)). ## Monthly P&L: the base case loses money because riders take most of each rupee

Assumptions for this model are labelled as such. All scenarios use 26 rider-days a month, and debits, COD bank charges and loss write-offs are set at ₹1 per parcel. The volumes come from the Indian last-mile study's average of **38.9 successful deliveries per rider per day** (out of 63 assigned) and its **80th-percentile load of 81 parcels** ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)), and from reports of 80-100 a day on dense Delhi-NCR beats ([India.com](https://www.india.com/business/delivery-boy-salary-how-much-do-amazon-flipkart-myntra-delivery-boys-make-each-successful-delivery-earns-them-rs-12-per-parcel-8031559/)). Rider costs are tied to sourced facts:

- **Minimum wage.** Telangana's Zone I minimum wage is **₹16,000 a month unskilled and ₹17,000 semi-skilled** from June 2026 ([HRengage](https://hrengage.ai/compliance/minimum-wages/telangana)).
- **ESI.** The employer pays 3.25% ([FactoHR](https://factohr.com/?p=49132)).
- **Fuel.** Petrol is ₹116.15 a litre in Hyderabad ([AajTak](https://www.aajtak.in/fuel-price/petrol-price-in-hyderabad-today)). My fuel estimate of ₹4,000-5,400 per rider per month also assumes 45 km/l, which is not sourced.

Overheads are sourced: Telangana commercial electricity runs ₹8.50-9.90 a unit ([electricbill.in](https://electricbill.in/en/tariffs/telangana)), broadband ₹699-799 ([Airtel](https://www.airtel.in/plans/broadband)), and GST filing about ₹399-999 a month ([MyDigitalFiling](https://mydigitalfiling.in/gst-return-filing-online/)).

The base case needs **about ₹22 per delivered parcel just to break even** (₹77,160 of costs ÷ 3,510 parcels). The owner-rides row is the uncomfortable one. In the base case, an owner who works full days as a rider takes home **₹3,570**, which is less than the ₹18,000-24,000 the same KPHB listing offers to a hired rider ([WorkIndia](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/delivery_boy-kphb-hyderabad-5986942/)). Only the optimistic case turns the hub into an income, and most of that income is the owner's own rider wage.

**Payback.** In the optimistic case, ₹16,220 a month repays ₹3 lakh in about 18.5 months. Counting only non-refundable spend and ramp-up losses, the figure is about 12 months. The base case never pays back. The pessimistic case burns through the roughly ₹2 lakh of working capital in about four months. The rent deposit and any genuine partner deposit can be recovered; the rest is lost.

## Break-even needs 115-175 parcels a day, which is 40-60 per rider

Break-even depends on just two numbers: the hub payout and the rider rate. The tables below use ₹18,000 a month of fixed costs (rent plus overheads, no optimizer) and ₹1 per parcel of debits. All figures are my arithmetic. At the end of break-even the owner still earns nothing for his time.

| Payout per parcel | Riders at ₹15/parcel | Riders at ₹13/parcel | Riders on salary (₹21,000 each incl. fuel and ESI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹14 | Never (hub keeps −₹2) | Never (₹0) | 240/day (80 per rider) |
| ₹18 | 346/day (beyond capacity) | 173/day | 183/day (61 per rider) |
| ₹20 | 173/day | 115/day | 164/day (55 per rider) |
| ₹22 | 115/day | 87/day | 148/day (49 per rider) |
| ₹25 | 77/day | 63/day | 130/day (43 per rider) |

**Three riders are enough to reach break-even. They are not enough to build a business.** The documented average is about 39 successful deliveries per rider per day. The heaviest day in the study was 129 ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). On that basis, three riders realistically deliver 120-150 a day, and 210-240 on mature, dense beats. That covers the break-even points only at ₹20 or more a parcel. The profit ceiling with three riders is about ₹16,000-25,000 a month even at ₹22. More riders help only if the network assigns more PIN codes, and the hub cannot control assigned volume ("DAs have no control over the quantity of parcels") ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). The ₹13 column also carries a legal risk. At 45 parcels a day, ₹13 a parcel works out to about ₹15,200 a month, below the ₹16,000 minimum wage if the riders count as employees. Per-parcel pay suits low or uncertain volume, because the cost falls when volume falls. Salaries work out cheaper once riders consistently exceed about 55-60 parcels a day.

## A route optimizer saves minutes, and costs more than it returns

Route optimization is the weakest part of the plan. The rider must still scan, call, collect OTP or COD and mark each status inside the carrier's own app. In the Indian study, riders were already free to choose their own stop order ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). So an external tool can only suggest a sequence, and someone would have to re-type 40-100 Indian landmark-style addresses per rider every morning. Shadowfax already markets in-app "SF Maps" sequencing ([Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.shadowfax.gandalf&hl=en_IN)). Copying customer details into a third-party app can also breach partner data terms; this was not verified.

Paid tools cost **$12-50 per driver per month** (Zeo about $24, OptimoRoute $35-39), and the free tiers stop at 10-20 stops ([EfiRoute](https://efiroute.com/blog/route-optimization-software-cost)). Vendor claims of 10-19% fewer miles have no published method ([FleetRabbit](https://fleetrabbit.com/industry/e-commerce-logistics/10-ways-route-optimization-software-cuts-delivery-costs); [Descartes](https://www.descartes.com/solutions/routing-mobile-and-telematics/route-optimization)). No independent field trial against experienced two-wheeler riders exists. Amazon's own routing research starts from the observation that experienced drivers knowingly depart from optimized plans ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-mit-team-up-to-add-driver-know-how-to-delivery-routing-models)).

The arithmetic is unforgiving. At 8.8 minutes per parcel and stops mostly under 500 m apart, riding is perhaps 1-3 minutes of each stop (my estimate). A 10-15% cut in riding time saves roughly 4-20 minutes per rider per day, worth 0.5-2 extra parcels, and only if the network has assigned them. At a ₹3-6 hub spread, that is worth **about ₹120-940 a month across three riders**, against **₹3,000-10,000** in subscriptions. Riders on per-parcel pay buy their own fuel, so the fuel saving goes to them.

The levers with evidence behind them work at the doorstep instead. Riders in the study logged **19.6 genuine failed attempts a day** and spent 59 minutes before their first attempt ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). Confirming addresses over WhatsApp reportedly cut RTO losses by 45% at Shiprocket, a vendor-reported figure ([Logistics Insider](https://www.logisticsinsider.in/india-address-problem-last-mile-delivery-digipin/)). Delhivery's shift from PIN-code to locality-based sorting raised last-mile productivity by **40-60%** ([arXiv 1802.04625](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04625)). Pre-calling customers, giving each rider a tight fixed cluster and pre-sorting in delivery order are all free. Each beats a paid optimizer.

## Scams, debits and COD cash are the risks that wipe out the capital

**Scams.** Fraud is the most likely way to lose the whole ₹3 lakh at once. XpressBees officially warns that impersonators are selling fake franchises by phone, WhatsApp and lookalike websites ([XpressBees](https://www.xpressbees.com/partner/franchise-partner)). Complainants report paying **₹50,000 to a "franchise contact"** with no response ([PissedConsumer](https://valmo.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)), and sending money to a "Valmo agent's" bank account ([Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/valmo-franchise-hub/254606)). Sites with near-Valmo names advertise "₹1-2 lakh" hub franchises ([valmalogistic.in](https://valmalogistic.in/franchise.php)). Their legitimacy is unverified, and Valmo's own domain is valmo.in. Police elsewhere have documented the same pattern, an "approved, now pay" message followed by the loss. One victim lost ₹5.72 lakh on a fake Campa Cola franchise ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fkarnataka-cen-police-bust-inter-state-cyber-fraud-racket-two-arrested-3815215)).

**Debits.** These can wipe out thin margins. Valmo's published delivery-service agreement requires **same-day COD deposits** and allows lost or damaged shipments to be **penalised at invoice value** ([Valmo agreement](https://www.valmo.in/static-assets/valmo-partner-app/documents/valmo-app-delivery-service-agreement-v1.0.html)). At a ₹2-6 spread, one lost ₹1,500 parcel erases the hub's margin on 250-750 deliveries. Fake "customer unavailable" marks are the largest single cause of returns in the Indian study ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). That makes rider supervision a financial control, not just an operational one.

**COD cash.** COD made up about **45% of parcels** in the study ([Arora et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.29080)). By my estimate, three riders could handle about ₹7.5 lakh of cash a month. That exceeds HDFC's ₹2 lakh free deposit limit, beyond which deposits cost ₹3.5-4 per ₹1,000 ([HDFC](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/content/dam/hdfcbankpws/in/en/personal-banking/discover-products/accounts/current-account/startup-current-account/startup-fees-and-charges.pdf)). Any rider who absconds with a day's cash is the owner's loss.

**Volume dependence.** This is structural and outside the owner's control. Meesho is moving volume to Valmo at lower cost per order than the third-party carriers charge ([Jefferies via X](https://x.com/nimeshscnbc/status/1995674991128723925)). Ecom Express sold to Delhivery at an 80% valuation cut ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/logistics-firm-delhivery-to-acquire-ecom-express-rs-1407-crore-deal-125040500406_1.html)), and Delhivery kept only 50-55% of Ecom's volume ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ecom-express-buyout-expands-delhiverys-market-share-by-25)). Shadowfax slipped on its listing over client concentration ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/27/indias-shadowfax-slips-on-listing-as-client-concentration-spooks-investors)).

**Riders.** Riders are non-exclusive and churn heavily. Shadowfax's RHP says its partners can switch to rivals for incentives ([Shadowfax RHP](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_red_herring_prospectus.pdf)), and rider job ads advertise ₹22,000-47,000 at high volume ([WorkIndia](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/delivery_boy-nizampet-hyderabad-8504350/)). A small hub competes for the same riders.

## Pilot cheaply, and sign a lease only after the rate and volume are in writing

The cheapest real test costs almost nothing. Amazon Hub Delivery needs **no upfront investment**: an existing documented shop delivers **25-50 packages within 2-3 km**, Amazon pays a fixed per-delivery amount monthly, and partners and their staff are insured ([About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/i-have-space-discover-an-extra-source-of-income-with-zero-investments)). Run it for 4-6 weeks from a relative's or a partner shop, with the owner riding. Alternatively, work 2-3 weeks as a per-parcel rider on a target PIN code. Either route reveals the true rate, the failure share, COD behaviour and daily volume for under ₹10,000 of fuel and time. Only then approach Valmo, Shadowfax, XpressBees, Ekart or Delhivery for a hub, through their official domains and local area managers, never through ads or WhatsApp agents. Pay nothing until a corporate-domain agreement exists, and pay deposits only into a company account against a receipt.

| Ask the partner, in writing | Why it decides the business |
|---|---|
| Rate card: forward delivery, reverse pickup, RTO and failed attempt, COD vs prepaid, weight and distance slabs, incentives | Below about ₹20 per delivered parcel, three hired riders cannot break even |
| Last 3 months' daily volume for the exact PIN codes, and whether an existing hub will be split | Break-even needs 115-175 parcels a day; the closed Valmo hub got 20-25 |
| Minimum volume guarantee or exclusivity for the PIN codes | None was found published; without one, all volume risk sits with you |
| Deposit amount, refund terms and timeline, and any onboarding fee | Determines whether ₹3 lakh leaves enough working capital |
| Settlement cycle and payout-dispute process | Monthly settlement means pre-funding 30-45 days |
| Debit schedule: lost or damaged (invoice value?), COD shortage, SLA penalties, caps, dispute window | One debit can erase weeks of margin |
| COD remittance method (rider UPI or QR straight to partner account?) | Removes cash custody, theft risk and bank charges |
| Who supplies scanners, printers, signage and shipment insurance | Can cut ₹20,000-40,000 from setup |
| Agreement term, notice period, rate-revision clause, policy on third-party route apps | Rates are trending down; check how fast they can change |

A go decision needs three things together: a written rate of at least ₹20-22 per delivered parcel, documented PIN-code volume of at least 150 a day, and a lean fit-out that keeps at least ₹1.5 lakh as working capital. Without all three, the money is safer elsewhere.

## Conclusion

The idea's premise, that software-driven rider productivity drives profit, gets the economics backwards. Volume and the per-parcel spread are set by the partner network, and the network's own margins are in paise. A hub owner's real levers are failed-attempt reduction, cluster design, cash control and rider supervision, and none of these needs a paid optimizer. On realistic numbers, a three-rider hub is closer to a self-employment arrangement than an investment. It pays reasonably only when the owner rides, the beat is dense and the rate is at the top of the unofficial band.

The decisive gap in all the evidence is that no network's actual rate card or PIN-code volume is public. That makes the first ₹10,000 spent on a pilot worth more than the remaining ₹2.9 lakh spent on a hub. If a partner will not put ₹20 or more a parcel and 150 or more parcels a day in writing, the safest answer is to keep the capital. If it will, start lean, start with the owner riding, and add hired riders only once each one is consistently delivering 55 or more parcels a day.
