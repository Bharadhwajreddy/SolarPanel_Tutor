# Case Studies: Indian Multi-Client Last-Mile / 3PL Delivery Operators (2022-2026)

Labels used throughout:
- **[VERIFIED]**: regulatory filing (DRHP, RoC data reported by Entrackr/Inc42) or mainstream business press reporting on filings or court records.
- **[COMPANY PR]**: company-published or press-release numbers, not audited.
- **[ANECDOTAL/BLOG]**: third-party blogs, franchise-guide sites or marketing content. Treat as indicative only.

Research note: about 19 tool calls. Several DRHP pages (ipocentral) returned 403, and The CapTable's 3PL piece is paywalled. So most DRHP figures below come from secondary coverage of the filing, not the filing text.

## 1. Examples of multi-client last-mile vendors and asset-light networks (Shadowfax, Loadshare, ElasticRun, LetsTransport, Zypp, EVeez, Blitz): scale, partner counts, how partners are paid

### Takeaway
The large "multi-carrier" operators that disclose numbers (Shadowfax, Zypp, Loadshare, LetsTransport) all use an asset-light, per-task-paid gig or fleet-partner model. They serve several platforms at once, but they run on very thin margins (Shadowfax EBITDA about 3.6%). Several (Loadshare, ElasticRun, LetsTransport, Zypp) were loss-making or shrinking in FY24-FY25. A small Hyderabad operator would be a sub-vendor or fleet partner inside this kind of ecosystem, not a competitor to it.

### Cited Findings

**Shadowfax (largest disclosed example; listed via IPO in Jan 2026)**
- [VERIFIED] H1 FY26 (Apr-Sep 2025) operating revenue was Rs 1,805.6 Cr, up 68% from Rs 1,072 Cr. Profit was about Rs 21 Cr (vs Rs 9.8 Cr) and EBITDA margin 3.56%; the company spent "about Rs 1 per Rs 1 of operating income". — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/shadowfax-reports-rs-1806-cr-revenue-in-h1-fy26-profits-double-10613200)
- [VERIFIED] Delivery personnel costs were Rs 956 Cr, about 53% of total expenses or about 53% of revenue. Other costs as a share of revenue were roughly: transportation Rs 325.2 Cr (about 18%), employee benefits Rs 171.8 Cr (about 9.5%), lost-shipment costs Rs 148.2 Cr (about 8.2%, more than 3x year on year) and other overheads Rs 197.4 Cr (about 11%). — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/shadowfax-reports-rs-1806-cr-revenue-in-h1-fy26-profits-double-10613200)
- [VERIFIED] Delivery partners number about 1.25 lakh, covering 14,000+ pin codes — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/shadowfax-reports-rs-1806-cr-revenue-in-h1-fy26-profits-double-10613200). Secondary DRHP summaries cite 205,864 *average quarterly transacting* partners as of 30 Sep 2025 and 3,000+ trucks a day (asset-light, leased) for middle mile — [IPO Central (search snippet)](https://ipocentral.in/shadowfax-ipo-udrhp-decoded/). These are different metrics: monthly active vs quarterly transacting.
- [VERIFIED, secondary summary] Partners are paid through an app-based, incentive-linked payout system, so supply can be matched to fluctuating demand. Partners are non-exclusive and can switch to other platforms when incentives are better, which the DRHP flags as a peak-season cost risk. — [IPO Central (search snippet)](https://ipocentral.in/shadowfax-ipo-udrhp-decoded/); [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/shadowfax-ipo-review)
- [VERIFIED] FY25 revenue was Rs 2,485 Cr (up 32%) with net profit Rs 6.4 Cr, a net margin of about 0.26%. Express parcels were about 69% of H1 FY26 revenue and hyperlocal/quick commerce Rs 359.3 Cr (about 20%, up 83%). — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/shadowfax-reports-rs-1806-cr-revenue-in-h1-fy26-profits-double-10613200); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ipo-bound-shadowfaxs-profit-surges-114-to-inr-21-cr-in-h1-fy26)
- [ANALYST ESTIMATE] Revenue per order is about Rs 57.9 against a cost of about Rs 55.7, leaving about Rs 2.2 operating profit per order (FY26 estimate). Riders cost about Rs 52 of every Rs 100 of revenue. — reported in search summary of [PL India](https://www.plindia.com/news/shadowfax-ipo-2000-cr-issue-flipkart-exit-profit-rebound-and-key-risks/) (analyst's own calculations, not a filing).
- [COMPANY HISTORY] Shadowfax started in 2015 doing food last-mile and used a crowdsourced partner app rather than full-time contracted riders. It then won Swiggy, Amazon, Flipkart and McDonald's as clients. — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/product-roadmap-shadowfax-logistics-swiggy-amazon-flipkart)

**Zypp Electric (EV fleet plus delivery-rider supplier to platforms)**
- [VERIFIED, RoC via Entrackr] FY25 operating revenue was Rs 438 Cr (FY24: Rs 293 Cr) and the loss was about Rs 107.5 Cr (FY24: Rs 89.5 Cr). Delivery services were 74% of revenue (Rs 323 Cr, up 56%) and vehicle rental income Rs 111 Cr. Rider expenses ("production, transportation and other operating") were Rs 355 Cr, more than the delivery revenue line. — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/zypp-electric-revenue-grows-50-in-fy25-losses-stands-at-rs-107-cr-11126259)
- [COMPANY PR] Pre-audited FY25 revenue was Rs 455 Cr (vs Rs 302 Cr). The company claims 1.2 lakh+ gig delivery partners served, an active fleet of 22,000+ EVs in Delhi NCR, Bengaluru and Mumbai, and 100 million deliveries; quick commerce was 47% of deliveries in FY25 (30% in FY24). — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/04/zypp-electric-revenue-jumps-rs-455-cr-fy25-eyes-ebitda-profitability-coming-quarters); [Tribune/ANI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/zypp-electric-closes-fy25-at-rs455-crore-reports-50-revenue-growth)
- Conflict: company Rs 455 Cr vs RoC Rs 438 Cr. A later report says Rs 448 Cr and a target of about Rs 600 Cr for FY26, plus IPO plans. — [EMobility+](https://emobilityplus.com/2025/11/26/zypp-electric-accelerates-toward-%e2%82%b9600-crore-revenue-ipo-plans-as-ev-fleet-expansion-gains-momentum/amp/)
- Clients: the sources found do not name Zomato or Zepto individually for Zypp.

**Loadshare Networks (asset-light network of local partners)**
- [VERIFIED, secondary] FY24 revenue was about Rs 348.1 Cr (down 12%) against total expenses of Rs 391.3 Cr (down 23%). The loss narrowed but the business shrank. FY23 scale was "flat". — [Inc42 financials](https://inc42.com/company/loadshare/financials/); [YourStory](https://cwv.yourstory.com/2024/11/tiger-global-backed-loadshare-loss-improves-revenue-declines-fy24); [Medial/Entrackr FY23](https://medial.app/news/loadshares-scale-remains-flat-in-fy23-losses-shrink-19percent-c10cd4b1569dd)
- Conflict: Affluense claims Rs 1,200 Cr FY24 revenue, which is inconsistent with the above; disregard. — [Affluense](https://www.affluense.ai/company/loadshare-networks-financials-545360)

**ElasticRun (rural/B2B distribution using local "run" partners)**
- [VERIFIED] FY24 GMV or revenue was about Rs 2,434 Cr, down 49% from Rs 4,738 Cr, and the net loss Rs 359.6 Cr (FY23: Rs 619 Cr). The company pivoted to regional and smaller brands. — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/elasticrun-gmv-drops-49-to-rs-2434-cr-in-fy24-cuts-losses-by-42-7371589); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/elasticruns-fy24-revenue-narrows-to-half-loss-declines-42)

**LetsTransport (aggregates truck/LCV fleet owners)**
- [VERIFIED, secondary] FY24 operating income was about Rs 390 Cr with a net loss of about Rs 114 Cr. Bertelsmann later acquired it. — [Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/bertelsmann-next-enters-india-with-acquisition-of-logistics-player-lets-transport)
- [DATABASE] As of July 2024 it had a network of 55,000+ trucks and served 200+ enterprises. — [Preqin](https://www.preqin.com/data/profile/asset/letstransport/172863)

**EVeez, Blitz**
- No revenue or partner-count disclosures were found in this research. EVeez is named only as a Zypp competitor. — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/zypp-electric-revenue-grows-50-in-fy25-losses-stands-at-rs-107-cr-11126259)

**Platforms' growing use of 3PLs**
- [VERIFIED, partial; paywalled] The CapTable (Jan 2025) reports that platforms are moving to a hybrid model, fulfilling part of their orders via 3PLs (Loadshare, Rapido, Shadowfax) rather than only in-house fleets. Context: Zomato had 6.25 lakh monthly active riders, and a 2023 strike shut 100+ of Blinkit's 400 Delhi-NCR dark stores. 3PLs act as capacity buffers. — [The CapTable](https://the-captable.com/2025/01/quick-commerce-delivery-3pl-loadshare-rapido-shadowfax/)

### Inferences
- In every disclosed case, the biggest cost is rider pay, at about 50-55% of revenue for Shadowfax and roughly 100%+ of delivery revenue for Zypp. Even at Rs 1,800 Cr scale, the operator keeps only low single digits. A small sub-vendor should expect its own gross spread (client rate minus rider payout) to be thin, and should plan around operating cost control rather than markup.
- Lost or damaged shipment cost (8% of revenue at Shadowfax) is a material line that small operators often ignore. For a parcel sub-vendor, COD and shipment-loss liability can wipe out the margin.
- The "multi-client" advantage in practice is filling idle rider hours (food or quick-commerce peaks plus e-commerce day slots), which is what Shadowfax and Zypp's quick-commerce mix describes.

### Gaps
- How Shadowfax, Loadshare and Zypp pay *sub-vendors or fleet partners* (as opposed to individual riders): the per-order or per-rider rate and settlement cycle were not found. They are private contract terms.
- Partner counts and economics for EVeez and Blitz were not found.

## 2. Margin structure, payment terms, receivables and working capital

### Takeaway
Filings show the scale players earn EBITDA margins of about 3-4% and carry debtors of about 36-48 days. A small vendor who pays riders weekly but is paid by clients monthly needs roughly 1.5-2 months of payroll as working capital. No authoritative public benchmark for small delivery-staffing vendor gross margin was found; per-order commission rates are private.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] Shadowfax's EBITDA margin was 3.56% in H1 FY26 and its FY25 net margin was about 0.26% (Rs 6.4 Cr on Rs 2,485 Cr). — [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/shadowfax-reports-rs-1806-cr-revenue-in-h1-fy26-profits-double-10613200)
- [VERIFIED, secondary] Shadowfax had trade receivables of Rs 373.43 Cr at 30 Sep 2025, about 20.7% of half-year revenue (roughly 37-38 days of revenue). — search summary of [INDmoney IPO page](https://www.indmoney.com/ipo/shadowfax-ipo)
- [DATA AGGREGATOR] Shadowfax debtor days were 40 (Mar 2023), 36 (Mar 2024) and 48 (Mar 2025). Working-capital days were negative (-18) because payables to partners also run on credit. — [Screener](https://www.screener.in/company/id/1285868/consolidated)
- [ANALYST ESTIMATE] Shadowfax makes about Rs 2.2 operating profit on about Rs 57.9 revenue per order (about 3.8%). — [PL India](https://www.plindia.com/news/shadowfax-ipo-2000-cr-issue-flipkart-exit-profit-rebound-and-key-risks/)
- [ANECDOTAL/BLOG] An illustrative vendor cost model puts delivery charges at Rs 35-50 per drop and platform commission at Rs 1,000-3,000 per route per month, plus quality-control and cash-handling costs. — [Spirestock blog](https://spirestock.com/blog/gig-workers-fmcg-distribution-india)
- [ANECDOTAL/BLOG] Rider base pay is typically Rs 20-50 per order within a fixed radius, plus daily-target incentives. 3PL contractor per-order rates change by city, season and company policy "without notice". — [Niftytrader](https://www.niftytrader.in/markets/gig-worker-earnings-unpacked-how-zomato-and-swiggy-delivery-partners-get-paid/); [Jobs24India](https://www.jobs24india.com/blog/delivery/delivery-boy-salary-in-india)
- [VERIFIED, press] In Nov 2025 quick-commerce rider earnings per order fell to Rs 15-27 from Rs 22-30 after Zepto and Instamart removed handling and surge fees. Riders now quit within 3-6 months, and platforms use order batching to cut cost. — [Financial Express via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Financial-Express-Chandigarh/FEE-CUT-PAIN-FOR-DELIVERY-PARTNERS); [Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/es/stories/newspaper/Mint-Bangalore/DARKSTORE-SURGE-CASTS-A-SHADOW-ON-RIDER-PAY)
- [VERIFIED, court record] Invoice discounters who had financed vendors' receivables on Dunzo filed insolvency pleas. NCLT dismissed one such plea, which shows that vendors were factoring platform invoices to bridge working capital. — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/amp/story/news/litigation/nclt-dismisses-insolvency-plea-by-invoice-discounters-against-dunzo)
- Upcoming cost pressure: the New Labour Codes (social-security contributions for gig and platform workers), expected around April 2026, are flagged as a cost risk for Shadowfax. — [Whalesbook](https://whalesbook.pages.dev/news/English/Transportation/Shadowfax-IPO-Risks-Gig-Worker-Woes-Threaten-indian-rupee7400-Cr-Valuation/6965f97748839f428387e002)

### Inferences
- Gross spread: if the large aggregator itself keeps only about 3-4% EBITDA after paying riders about 53%, the per-order rate it passes to a sub-vendor leaves the sub-vendor only a modest spread above rider pay. A planning assumption of about 8-15% gross spread for a staffing or sub-vendor, before the vendor's own overheads, is reasonable. This is an inference, not a sourced figure.
- Working capital: debtor days of 36-48 at a listed company suggest a small vendor should model 30-60-day receivables. If riders are paid weekly, about 1.5-2 months of rider payroll plus fuel or EV rent must be funded upfront. For example, 20 riders x Rs 18,000-22,000 a month means about Rs 5.5-9 lakh of working capital (illustrative).
- Rate cuts flow down: the Nov 2025 quick-commerce payout cuts show that when platforms cut fees, rider pay is cut within weeks. A vendor with fixed salaried riders absorbs this; one with per-order riders passes it on but suffers attrition.

### Gaps
- No public filing gives the gross margin of a *small* delivery-staffing vendor. Tracxn and Entrackr cover only funded startups.
- Exact payment terms (net 30/45/60) in the Amazon DSP, Flipkart Ekart vendor or Shadowfax sub-vendor contracts were not found publicly.

## 3. Amazon Delivery Service Partner (DSP) India: number of DSPs, fleet sizes, owner experiences, investment

### Takeaway
Amazon India's DSP count has stayed at about 300 businesses since 2021, with about 100 new DSPs added in 2021-23. Official owner stories show growth takes years: one station to 45 stations in about 8-9 years (M&M Logistics), and six stations with about 300 associates in about 5 years (Kerala). Amazon publishes no investment figure; blog estimates range from Rs 1.5 lakh to Rs 40 lakh and are unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- [COMPANY PR] At the Aug 2021 relaunch ("DSP 2.0") Amazon had 300+ entrepreneurs managing 1,500 delivery stations in 750 cities, and said entrepreneurs "with little to no delivery experience" can start. — [Amazon press](https://press.aboutamazon.com/in/2021/8/amazon-launches-next-evolution-of-its-delivery-service-partner-program-in-india); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/08/amazon-launches-new-delivery-service-partner-programme-india)
- [COMPANY PR] In Oct 2023 Amazon had about 300 SMB DSPs and close to 100 new DSPs onboarded in two years. The network had about 2,000 delivery stations in 750+ cities, and 12 new SMBs were added in North India in one batch. — [IndiaRetailing](https://www.indiaretailing.com/?p=169909); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/amazon-onboards-12-smbs-under-delivery-service-partner-programme)
- [COMPANY PR, case study] Sachin and Manisha Sharma founded M&M Logistics Solutions in Oct 2015 with one delivery station in Delhi. Sachin came from a family warehouse business, and a friend introduced him to DSP. They now run 45 stations in 21 cities across UP, Haryana, Uttarakhand and Delhi-NCR. Investment and headcount are not disclosed, and challenges are not specified. — [About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/meet-the-power-couple-running-45-delivery-stations-in-21-cities-across-north-india)
- [COMPANY PR, case study] Brijesh Kumar (Gods Own BA, Kerala) became a DSP in 2017. In about five years he built six stations (Palakkad, Kalpetta, Kodungallur and others) with about 300 delivery associates, including an all-women station with 40+ women. That works out to about 50 associates per station. — [About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/this-amazon-delivery-services-partner-is-creating-jobs-in-kerala)
- [ANECDOTAL/BLOG] Amazon provides uniforms and handheld devices; the DSP hires and trains associates and manages vehicles. DSP is a 3PL service contract, not a franchise. — [CostGuide](https://costguide.in/amazon-delivery-franchise-cost-in-india/); [OnlineLegalIndia](https://www.onlinelegalindia.com/blogs/how-to-become-amazon-logistics-partner/)
- [ANECDOTAL/BLOG] Investment estimates conflict widely: Rs 1.5 lakh minimum or Rs 30-40 lakh total ([GrowthJockey](https://www.growthjockey.com/blogs/amazon-franchise)); from Rs 15 lakh to more than Rs 40 lakh ([CostGuide](https://costguide.in/amazon-delivery-franchise-cost-in-india/)); Rs 6-8 lakh in liquid assets ([FranchiseHurt](https://franchisehurt.com/amazon-delivery-franchise-india/)). None of these were confirmed by Amazon.

### Inferences
- A typical Amazon DSP station seems to run about 30-60 associates (from the Kerala ratio of 300 associates across 6 stations), so a single DSP station is already at the "20-100 riders" target size.
- Amazon DSP contracts are typically *exclusive to Amazon at that station*. That makes DSP a single-client business, the opposite of the multi-carrier model, though an entrepreneur could run a DSP entity alongside a separate multi-client entity.
- The flat DSP count (about 300 in both 2021 and 2023) means new slots are limited and selective. Hyderabad entry depends on Amazon opening a new station or replacing a partner.

### Gaps
- No official Amazon figure on DSP investment, per-package or per-route payout, or DSP profit margins in India.
- No independent (non-Amazon) DSP owner interviews or complaints from India were found in this pass. Most coverage is Amazon PR.

## 4. Stories of operators who started with 2-5 riders and grew to 20-100+ serving multiple clients

### Takeaway
No credible, sourced public story was found of an individual who grew from 2-5 riders to 20-100 riders serving multiple platforms. The documented growth stories are Amazon-PR DSP cases (single client) and venture-funded companies. This gap is itself a signal: small multi-client vendors are informal and rarely covered.

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search ("started courier delivery business with 5 riders grew to 100 riders Flipkart Amazon Shadowfax vendor") returned only Shadowfax company history and franchise listicles, with no individual operator stories. — [StartupTalky franchise list](https://startuptalky.com/courier-franchise-businesses/); [YourStory on Shadowfax](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/product-roadmap-shadowfax-logistics-swiggy-amazon-flipkart)
- [COMPANY PR] The closest documented growth trajectories are DSP owners: 1 station (2015) to 45 stations (about 2023-24) for M&M Logistics, and about 6 stations with 300 associates in about 5 years for Gods Own BA. — [About Amazon India – M&M](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/meet-the-power-couple-running-45-delivery-stations-in-21-cities-across-north-india); [About Amazon India – Kerala](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/this-amazon-delivery-services-partner-is-creating-jobs-in-kerala)
- [VERIFIED] Shadowfax's own path ran from food-delivery last mile (2015) to Flipkart partnership (about 2018) to e-commerce express, which shows a "start with one vertical, then add a big e-commerce anchor" pattern. — [Shadowfax blog (company)](https://www.shadowfax.in/blogs/a-decade-of-delivering-dreams-shadowfaxs-10th-anniversary-celebration)

### Inferences
- A realistic path, inferred from the patterns above rather than sourced, would run in four stages:
  1. Join as an individual or small fleet sub-vendor to one aggregator, such as a Shadowfax, Zypp or Loadshare hub or a quick-commerce 3PL.
  2. Add a second client whose peak hours differ, for example e-commerce daytime plus food or quick-commerce evenings.
  3. Reach about 20-50 riders within 1-2 years.
  4. Only then apply for an anchor contract such as an Amazon DSP station or Ekart/Delhivery last-mile, which are multi-year build-outs.
- Rider attrition of 3-6 months in quick commerce means "20 riders" requires continuous recruitment of roughly 4-7 new riders a month to hold headcount (inference).

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn or YouTube founder interviews of small Hyderabad or Indian multi-client delivery vendors were retrieved in the tool budget. A dedicated YouTube search in Hindi or Telugu (for example "delivery vendor business Flipkart Shadowfax") may surface anecdotal stories.

## 5. Failure cases: client rate cuts, receivable delays (Dunzo, Ecom Express), quick-commerce payment disputes

### Takeaway
The best-documented failures are about **client collapse or concentration**, not operations. Dunzo left vendors unpaid, with insolvency pleas over amounts of Rs 2.5-11 Cr. Ecom Express shut about 1,000 delivery centres after its main client (Meesho) moved volume in-house. Quick-commerce fee removals in Nov 2025 cut per-order rider earnings by about 25-30%.

### Cited Findings
**Dunzo (2023-2025)**
- [VERIFIED] Seven companies sent legal notices over unpaid dues, about Rs 11.4 Cr combined (Google India, Facebook India, Nilenso, Clover Ventures, Cupshup, Koo, Glance). — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=407583); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/news/business/dunzo-faces-legal-notice-from-7-firms-over-unpaid-dues-726303)
- [VERIFIED] Betterplace Safety Solutions filed an IBC application (Feb 2024) for about Rs 4 Cr for background verification, *recruitment of delivery staff*, asset management and merchandise. This is the most direct example of a delivery-workforce vendor left unpaid. NCLT later forfeited Dunzo's right to reply. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/nclt-warns-dunzo-of-moratorium-over-unpaid-dues-worth-inr-4-cr); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/nclt-pulls-up-cash-strapped-dunzo-for-failing-to-reach-settlement-with-creditors)
- [VERIFIED] NCLT admitted Velvin Packaging's insolvency plea against Dunzo (about Rs 2.5 Cr per a startup database). — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/nclt-admits-velvin-packaging-solutions-insolvency-plea-against-dunzo); amount from [Lapaas Voice](https://voice.lapaas.com/startup/dunzo/business-model/) (less reliable)
- [VERIFIED] Dunzo deferred salaries repeatedly, a $22-25M funding deal collapsed in 2024, and it cut 75% of staff. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/cash-strapped-dunzo-defers-payment-of-pending-salaries-by-another-4-months); [Digital Terminal](https://digitalterminal.in/amp/story/trending/dunzo-lays-off-75-of-employees-to-bring-down-workforce-to-50)
- No reporting quantified unpaid payouts to *independent delivery-partner vendors* (sub-3PLs) at Dunzo.

**Ecom Express (2024-2025)**
- [VERIFIED] The DRHP showed one customer, most likely Meesho, at 52% of revenue, up from 29% in FY22. Meesho launched in-house logistics (Valmo) in early 2024 and by Dec 2024 had cut Ecom Express volume by about 40-50%. Amazon and Reliance also reduced orders. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/what-triggered-ecom-express-165-mn-fire-sale-to-delhivery)
- [VERIFIED] FY24 revenue grew only 2.3% to Rs 2,653 Cr. The 9M FY25 net loss was Rs 398 Cr and Jan-Mar 2025 revenue fell about 30% year on year. About 1,000 delivery centres were shut, 500+ employees laid off (Feb 2025), and the workforce was about 50,000, mostly part-time delivery partners. It had suffered a similar shock when Shopee exited India in 2022. — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/what-triggered-ecom-express-165-mn-fire-sale-to-delhivery)
- [VERIFIED] Delhivery bought 99.4% of Ecom Express for about Rs 1,407 Cr cash in Apr 2025, a distress sale cleared by CCI. — [Delhivery press](https://www.delhivery.com/press-release/delhivery-acquires-ecom-express); [BW Legal World](https://www.bwlegalworld.com/article/cci-clears-delhiverys-rs1407-cr-acquisition-of-ecom-express-in-distress-sale-560450)
- [ANECDOTAL/BLOG] Ecom Express's franchise programmes ("Atom Franchise", "SATHi Delivery Partner") are no longer offered after the acquisition. — [Linkdot](https://linkdot.io/ecom-express-franchise-cost/)
- Outlook Business reported that "unpaid dues and liabilities" made Delhivery reconsider deal risks, but no amounts were given. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/ampstories/news/delhiverys-ecom-express-deal-in-trouble-10-key-reasons-behind-the-roadblock)

**Quick-commerce payout cuts (2025)**
- [VERIFIED, press] Zepto and Instamart removed handling and surge fees in Nov 2025, and per-order rider earnings fell from Rs 22-30 to Rs 15-27. — [Financial Express via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Financial-Express-Chandigarh/FEE-CUT-PAIN-FOR-DELIVERY-PARTNERS)
- [VERIFIED, press] The government directed aggregators to drop the 10-minute delivery mandate, and UBS cut quick-commerce EBITDA estimates by 15-20% for FY26-27. — [Asianet Newsable](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/amp/business/quick-commerce-giants-face-margin-cuts-after-10min-delivery-rule-gone-articleshow-16hfh08)
- [VERIFIED] Reliance shut its JioMart Express quick-commerce delivery arm (date unverified in snippet). — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=384355)

### Inferences
- Failure modes for a small vendor, ranked by evidence:
  1. A client goes bust or delays payment (Dunzo).
  2. A client in-sources or shifts volume (Meesho to Valmo, hitting Ecom Express and its sub-vendors).
  3. A platform cuts per-order rates (quick commerce, Nov 2025).
  4. An aggregator is acquired and its partner programme is discontinued (Ecom Express franchises).
- Every Ecom Express delivery centre that closed likely hit local vendors and riders, but no sub-vendor-level reporting exists.

### Gaps
- No documented case of a named *small* last-mile vendor exiting after a rate cut was found.
- No quantified Ecom Express vendor or franchise dues were found.

## 6. Concentration risk: what share of revenue from one client is dangerous

### Takeaway
Even the listed winners are highly concentrated: Shadowfax gets about 49% from Flipkart and 74.6% from its top 5. Ecom Express shows what happens when a single client reaches 52% and leaves. Investors call it a "red flag". A practical rule from these cases is to keep any one client below about 30-40% of revenue once you are past the start-up stage (inference).

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, secondary DRHP summaries] Shadowfax's largest client (Flipkart Internet) contributed 48.91% of operating revenue in H1 FY26 (about Rs 883 Cr) and has stayed above 48% in recent periods. - [VERIFIED] Shadowfax lists client concentration as its top risk factor, alongside non-exclusive gig workers and leased facilities. — [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/ipo/shadowfax-ipo-review)

### Inferences
- For a small Hyderabad multi-client vendor:
  - Year 0-1: 60-100% from one anchor client is unavoidable.
  - Year 2: the target is to bring the largest client below about 50%.
  - From then on: keep the largest client below about 30-40% and have at least 3 active clients, with no two in the same parent group (Flipkart, Ekart and Shadowfax are linked, since Flipkart is Shadowfax's investor and top client).
  These thresholds are inferred from the Ecom Express collapse and investor commentary, not from a formal standard.
- Clients related to each other count as one exposure. Serving Shadowfax for Flipkart volume *and* Ekart directly is effectively Flipkart concentration.

### Gaps
- No Indian lender or credit-rating guideline giving a specific single-client threshold for small logistics vendors was found. Rating agencies (CRISIL/ICRA) for logistics SMEs may have one, but these were not searched.
