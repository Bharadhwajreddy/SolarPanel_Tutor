# Parcel / E-commerce Delivery Demand in India and Hyderabad: Volumes, Market Structure, Seasonality (2024-2026)

Research date: 6 Nov 2026. Fiscal years (FY) run April-March (FY25 = Apr 2024-Mar 2025). Two kinds of number appear below. **Cited** numbers come from a source. **Derived** numbers are arithmetic on cited numbers, and the method is always shown. The best primary source found was the Redseer "India 3rd Party Logistics Market" industry report in Shadowfax's IPO offer documents, dated Jan 2026 ([PDF](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)). In Redseer's definition, a "shipment" counts delivered parcels, RTO parcels (forward and return legs counted once) and reverse pickups. It covers e-commerce, D2C, social commerce and omnichannel, and it **excludes grocery and quick commerce**.

## 1. India e-commerce shipment volumes, growth and Tier-2/3 share

### Takeaway
India's e-commerce logistics (excluding grocery) handled **4.9-5.3 billion shipments in FY25, about 13.4-14.5 million a day**. Volume grew 28-31% CAGR over FY20-25. Redseer projects 23-27% CAGR to 15-16 billion by FY30, which puts FY26 at roughly 6.0-6.7 billion (16-18 million a day). Tier-2+ cities produce about **62% of shipments** but only 55% of GMV. On top of this sit quick commerce (1.1-1.3 billion FY25) and food delivery (1.8-2 billion FY25).

### Cited Findings
- FY25 e-commerce logistics (ex-grocery) shipments: 4.9-5.3 bn. FY20-25 CAGR 28-31%. FY30 projection 15-16 bn (23-27% CAGR). India has 3-4 shipments per capita versus 75-85 in China and 60-70 in the USA. — [Redseer industry report for Shadowfax RHP](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- Tier-2+ cities contributed ~55% of e-commerce GMV and **~62% of total shipments** in FY25. Low-AOV categories (fashion, beauty and personal care) make up ~85% of shipments. Average shipment value fell at ~4-6% CAGR over FY20-25. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- Quick commerce: 1.1-1.3 bn shipments in FY25, projected to grow at 29-44% CAGR to FY30. About 15% of quick commerce shipments go to 3PLs. Other on-demand hyperlocal (food plus others) is 2-2.2 bn shipments, about 92% of it food. Online food delivery alone is 1.8-2 bn shipments. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- Online retail is projected to grow at 20-25% CAGR over FY25-30. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- A separate Redseer article (Sep 2025) projects parcel volumes growing 15-20% CAGR and "crossing 1 billion parcels per month by 2030". That implies about 12 bn a year, **lower than the 15-16 bn in the RHP report**. The scope or the scenario probably differs. — [Redseer, "A billion parcels a month"](https://redseer.com/articles/a-billion-parcels-a-month-the-new-indian-3pl-landscape/)
- RedSeer is also quoted (via an I-Sec note) at ~5.1 bn total e-commerce shipments in FY25, which is consistent with the above. — [Scribd copy of Shadowfax research report](https://www.scribd.com/document/1016296177/Shadowfax-Research-Report)
- Bain "How India Shops Online 2025": Tier-2 and smaller cities provide ~60% of new customers since 2020 and ~45% of orders since 2023. The 2026 edition reports India e-retail crossing about USD 65-66 bn in 2025, with Tier-2+ giving ~50% of incremental orders in 2025. Shopper penetration is 25-30% of internet users in Tier-2+ versus 45-50% in metros and Tier-1. — [Bain 2025](https://bain.com/insights/how-india-shops-online-2025); [Bain 2026](https://www.bain.com/ja/insights/how-india-shops-online-2026); [Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/?p=2191111); [BW Retail](https://bwretailworld.com/sector/e-commerce-marketplaces/indias-e-retail-market-crosses-65-bn-set-for-20-growth)
- Unicommerce: Tier-2/3 cities accounted for 66% of incremental D2C order volume in FY26. D2C order volume grew 34% and GMV 33% in FY26. The base is 410M+ shipments from 6,000+ brands. — [Unicommerce India D2C Report 2026](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)
- Unicommerce, first week of the Oct 2026 festive sales: Tier-III towns alone accounted for 42% of e-commerce orders. — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/business/festive-shopping-e-commerce-quick-commerce-beauty-products-259554)
- Meesho shipped 158.7 crore (1.59 bn) orders in FY25, up 38% YoY. Meesho is about 29-31% of all Indian e-commerce shipments (Redseer, via Meesho IPO coverage). — [Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/inside-meeshos-ipo); [Axis/BS Meesho note](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2026-01/17679504020.34145800.pdf)
- Older, weaker estimates put daily volume at about 8M a day (GLG), or 12-14M a day (a statistics aggregator). Prefer the Redseer figure. — [GLG](https://glg.com/articles/analyzing-the-indian-e-commerce-logistics-market); [SaaSUltra](https://www.saasultra.com/india-ecommerce-statistics-2026/)

### Inferences
- Shipments grow faster than GMV because average order value keeps falling: low-AOV fashion and beauty, value commerce (Meesho), and more returns.

- For FY26, if an operator assumes the lower end of growth, 20-23% (Unicommerce D2C +34%, Unicommerce Diwali 2025 +24%, Delhivery +43% YoY in Q3FY26 with acquisition effects) would be a defensible base case.

## 2. Last-mile carrier market shares by shipment volume (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
In FY25, **captive logistics (Ekart, Amazon Transportation, Valmo and others) carried about 58-60%** of e-commerce shipments and **3PLs about 40-42%**. Within 3PL, H1 FY26 is now a near duopoly: **Delhivery (with Ecom Express) about 46% and Shadowfax about 23%** of 3PL e-commerce shipments. Meesho's Valmo is the fastest-growing captive network. It carried about 15% of all Indian e-commerce shipments in FY25 and probably about 20% in FY26 (derived).

### Cited Findings
- In FY25, 3PLs handled 40-42% of total e-commerce shipments. Horizontal marketplaces use their captive arms for 66-67% of their shipments and outsource 33-34%. Non-horizontal platforms (vertical and D2C, ~18% of shipments) outsource ~74% to 3PLs. Blue Dart, Delhivery, Shadowfax and Xpressbees "drive majority share" of 3PL e-commerce volume. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- Shadowfax's share of 3PL e-commerce shipments grew from ~8% in FY22 to **~23% in H1 FY26**. Shadowfax handled 228.4M express shipments in H1 FY26 (H1 FY25: 160.0M) plus 66.0M hyperlocal orders. Delhivery handled **453M express shipments in H1 FY26** (H1 FY25: 368M). Pin-code reach was 14,758 for Shadowfax and 18,830 for Delhivery. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- The CCI approved Delhivery's acquisition of Ecom Express in June 2025. — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- As of June 2025, Delhivery carried close to half of traditional e-commerce monthly shipments (excluding quick commerce; the context implies the 3PL universe). Shadowfax gained about 8 percentage points of share between Jul 2024 and Jul 2025 to become #2. — [Redseer article](https://redseer.com/articles/a-billion-parcels-a-month-the-new-indian-3pl-landscape/)
- In an analyst view, Delhivery holds about 46% and Shadowfax about 26% of 3PL express volume, together roughly 72-75%. — [Mann's Substack (JM Financial-linked)](https://mannashar.substack.com/p/shadowfax-tech)
- Delhivery reported 295M express parcel shipments in Q3 FY26 (Oct-Dec 2025), up 43% YoY and 20% QoQ. Q2 FY26 was 246M, Q3 FY25 206M, and 9M FY26 748M. Delhivery called it a "record festive season ... driven by strong SOW gains across clients". — [Delhivery Q3FY26 earnings presentation](https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2026/01/EarningsPresentation_Q3FY26.pdf)
- Meesho's Valmo share of Meesho shipped orders was 1.83% in FY23, 19.55% in FY24, **48.08% in FY25** and **64.52% in H1 FY26** (another note gives ~67% in Q2 FY26). Valmo handled 76.3 crore (763M) shipments in FY25, up from 22.4 crore in FY24. Valmo's cost per shipment is reportedly 1-11% below 3PL (Jefferies). — [Axis Capital/Business Standard Meesho note](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2026-01/17679504020.34145800.pdf); [Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/inside-meeshos-ipo); [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/transportation/Meesho-IPO-Opens-Today-Is-Valmos-Logistics-Surge-Secretly-Threatening-Delhiverys-Dominance/692fd76765a9badb9b6c94e8)
- A conflicting framing: in express parcels, Amazon Transportation, Ekart and Delhivery each hold 33%, 33% and 20% (post-Ecom), and Delhivery has ~16% of all online shipments (Equitec brokerage, Aug 2025). — [Equitec note via Business Standard](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2025-09/17567186470.65777600.pdf)
- Blue Dart reportedly handles about 8% of India's e-commerce shipments (2024; weak source). Wikipedia cites Ekart at over 6M daily shipments (undated). Xpressbees claims range from 1.2M a day (Q1 2025, ET via Clickpost) to "3M+" (its own site). — [Wikipedia Blue Dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Dart_Express); [Wikipedia Ekart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekart); [Clickpost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/xpressbees-courier-charges)
- Mordor Intelligence says the combined Delhivery and Ecom entity has about 35% "e-commerce share" and the top five players hold about 48% of third-party parcel volume. Its methodology is unclear. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-last-mile-delivery-market)

## 3. Hyderabad / Telangana: demand share, daily parcels, hubs and growth areas

### Takeaway
No source gives Hyderabad's share of national e-commerce orders or its daily parcel count. A top-down **assumption of 3-4% of national shipments puts Hyderabad at roughly 0.5-0.7 million e-commerce parcels a day in FY26**. Quick commerce is a strong indicator of density: Hyderabad has **484 dark stores, the third-largest count in India** (CLSA, Aug 2026). The main logistics nodes are **Shamshabad (airport, south), Medchal (north) and Patancheru (north-west)**.

### Cited Findings
- Hyderabad has 484 dark stores, behind Bengaluru (735) and Delhi (531) and ahead of Mumbai (372), Chennai (365) and Pune (293). Zepto leads in Hyderabad. The five platforms run 3,536 stores across the 10 cities, excluding Amazon and JioMart. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/flipkart-minutes-overtakes-swiggy-instamart-in-dark-stores-across-top-10-cities); [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/flipkart-minutes-overtakes-instamart-dark-stores-across-top-10-cities/)
- Delhivery moved from one Hyderabad hub at Shamshabad to two by adding **Medchal**. Andhra Pradesh and Telangana distribution both runs out of Hyderabad facilities (Q2 FY24 call, Nov 2023). — [Delhivery Q2FY24 earnings call transcript](https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2023/11/EarningsCallTranscript_Q2FY24.pdf)
- Amazon in Telangana (2019) had about 90 delivery stations (own and delivery service partner), 2 sort centres in Hyderabad (100,000 sq ft combined), 3 fulfilment centres, direct delivery to 500+ PIN codes, and 2,500+ "I Have Space" partners. It had just opened a 20,000 sq ft delivery centre in Ranga Reddy district, its largest in Telangana. Amazon's largest fulfilment centre is at Shamshabad (GMR Airport City). — [Retail4Growth (2019)](https://www.retail4growth.com/news/lite/amazon-opens-telanganas-largest-delivery-centre-in-hyderabad-4444); [Sakshi Post](https://m.sakshipost.com/amp/amazons-biggest-fulfillment-center-in-hyderabad-to-enable-faster-delivery)
- Ecom Express set up a grocery fulfilment centre at Medchal, with 800,000 throughput capacity (older article). — [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/ecom-express-sets-up-grocery-fulfilment-centre-at-medchal)
- Hyderabad warehousing transactions were about 3.4M sq ft in 2025, versus 3.5M in 2024 (-5%). 3PL and manufacturing made up 66% of leasing (Knight Frank). Vestian puts 2025 absorption at 2.2M sq ft (-31%) with rents of Rs 20.8/sq ft/month (+10%). Patancheru and Medchal are emerging as logistics nodes. Shamshabad benefits from the airport. — [Hans India](https://assets.thehansindia.com/business/hyd-warehousing-transactions-touch-34-mn-sq-ft-in-2025-1054422); [RP Realty Plus](https://www.rprealtyplus.com/news-views/indias-warehousing-absorption-falls-14-in-2025-despite-strong-growth-123899.html); [Propertywala](https://propertywala.com/blog/trends/hyderabads-logistics-boom-transforms-peripheral-micro-markets-into-investment-hotspots)
- Courier aggregator guidance: brands shipping more than 500 orders a day often use Shamshabad for national outbound and Gachibowli for intra-city speed. Medchal and Shamshabad are cited as the linehaul hubs (non-authoritative). — [Clickpost Hyderabad](https://www.clickpost.ai/courier-service-companies-in-india/hyderabad); [OneXShip](https://www.onexship.in/courier-services/telangana/hyderabad)
- Hyderabad traders reported that online shopping drew festive (Dasara) demand away from shops in Oct 2025. This is anecdotal. — [Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/news/cities/hyderabad/spurt-in-online-shopping-cripples-festive-retail-trade-in-hyd-1011939)
- Early quick-commerce micro-warehouses in north Hyderabad included Swiggy at Lothkunta (about 5 km radius), plus others near Kompally and Alwal (2021). — [Telangana Today (2021)](https://telanganatoday.com/quick-commerce-wave-catching-on)

## 4. Seasonality: month-by-month volume curve

### Takeaway
No published India monthly e-commerce volume index was found. Anchored on Delhivery's quarterly volumes and October 2025 data, an indicative curve runs **Apr-Jun at a base of 100, Jul-Aug about 105-110, September about 130 as festive sales start, October about 150-160 at peak, Nov-Dec about 130-135, and Jan-Mar about 95-105**. The single peak day is about 2.2x the Q1 average day.

### Cited Findings
- Delhivery Q1 FY26 (Apr-Jun) ~207M, Q2 FY26 (Jul-Sep) 246M (+19% QoQ), Q3 FY26 (Oct-Dec) 295M (+20% QoQ, "record festive season"). — [Delhivery Q3FY26 presentation](https://www.delhivery.com/uploads/2026/01/EarningsPresentation_Q3FY26.pdf)
- Delhivery handled over 107 million e-commerce and freight shipments in October 2025, with a **single-day peak of 7.2M**. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/delhivery-moves-over-19-000-crore-in-goods-amid-record-festive-surge-125110301088_1.html)
- Unicommerce: the 2025 Diwali festive season saw order volume up 24% YoY and GMV up 23%, across 150M+ transactions. — [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/unicommerce-reports-24-order-volume-growth-during-2025-diwali-festive-season/); [Business News This Week](https://businessnewsthisweek.com/business/2025-diwali-festive-season-concludes-with-24percent-order-volume-growth-unicommerce/)
- Unicommerce, first week of the 2026 festive sales: e-commerce orders up 31% YoY, quick commerce up 85% YoY, average order value down 10%. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/ecom-order-volumes-rise-31-in-first-week-of-festive-sales-unicommerce-126101401136_1.html); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/festive-sales-surge-31-quick-commerce-orders-jump-85-report-4182694)
- A conflicting 2026 report says dispatch volumes on major marketplaces are tracking 10-12% below last year, attributed to electronics price hikes. This is a weak source. — [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/consumer-products/India-Festive-E-commerce-Sales-Dip-12percent-Amid-Price-Hikes/6ad5923579a16deaeaf63ceb)
- Datum Intelligence forecasts about 966M festive-period orders in 2026, versus about 760M in 2025. EasyEcom says festive demand is spreading over a longer window. — [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/business/festive-shopping-e-commerce-quick-commerce-beauty-products-259554)
- Redseer: captive networks rely on 3PLs for peak periods, and the year has "10+ indices of sales". — [Redseer/Shadowfax report](https://www.shadowfax.in/investor-relations/offer-documents/shadowfax_india_3rd_party_logistics_market_industry_report.pdf)
- Unicommerce D2C RTO was 39.2% at the Nov 2025 festive peak, falling to 25.6% in Jan 2026 and 21.0% in Mar 2026 (for "optimised brands"). Returns therefore peak in Nov-Dec, after forward volume. — [Unicommerce D2C Report 2026](https://unicommerce.com/india-d2c-report-2026-april/)
- Global proxy (not India): January-April averages about 18% fewer orders than the annual baseline. — [Syncio](https://www.syncio.co/blog/ecommerce-seasonality)

## 5. COD vs prepaid, RTO rates, AOV, parcel weight

### Takeaway
COD still dominates Indian e-commerce at **about 60-65% of orders** nationally (higher in Tier-2+ and value commerce, lower in metros, where Shiprocket shows Delhi at about 40% COD). Prepaid is growing faster. **RTO is about 20-30% for COD versus about 2% for prepaid** (indicative). Average order value is falling (by about 10% YoY in the 2026 festive season). No reliable average parcel weight was found.

### Cited Findings
- COD is about 60-65% of all Indian e-commerce orders (ET Prime Research), about 70% of D2C orders (Shiprocket-KPMG), and about 90% of transactions in Tier-2 to Tier-4 cities (Shiprocket), all as cited by a secondary blog. — [The Growth Agency](https://www.thegrowthagency.in/blogs/cod-rto-cost-india)
- Shiprocket Trends for Delhi: 60% prepaid, 40% COD, and RTO 12%. — [Shiprocket Trends](https://trends.shiprocket.in/)
- Unicommerce, Diwali 2025: prepaid orders grew 26% YoY, COD orders 22% by volume and 35% by GMV. — [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/unicommerce-reports-24-order-volume-growth-during-2025-diwali-festive-season/)

These come from blogs with unclear methodology. — [The Growth Agency](https://www.thegrowthagency.in/blogs/cod-rto-cost-india); [Amazon Shipping blog](https://shipping.amazon.in/blog/cod-vs-prepaid-festive-season-surge-india)

## 6. Parcels per delivery centre / per PIN code / per rider

## 7. Quick commerce in Hyderabad as alternative rider demand

### Takeaway
Quick commerce is the fastest-growing delivery pool: 1.1-1.3 bn shipments nationally in FY25, growing 29-44% CAGR, and +85% YoY in the 2026 festive week. **Hyderabad has 484 dark stores (Aug 2026), third in India**, and Zepto leads there. Only about 15% of quick commerce is outsourced to 3PLs, so rider supply to platforms or Shadowfax-style partners is the realistic entry point.

### Cited Findings
- Hyderabad has 484 dark stores (CLSA, Aug 2026), and Zepto leads in the city. Across 10 cities: Blinkit 969, Zepto 828, Flipkart Minutes 627, Instamart 615. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/flipkart-minutes-overtakes-swiggy-instamart-in-dark-stores-across-top-10-cities); [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/flipkart-minutes-overtakes-instamart-dark-stores-across-top-10-cities/)
