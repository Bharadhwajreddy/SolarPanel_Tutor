# Delivery-Rider Economics and Productivity for E-commerce Last-Mile Delivery in Hyderabad (2025-2026)

Research date: 6 Nov 2026. Most rider pay data comes from job-portal listings (WorkIndia, JobHai, Indeed, Vahan) and career blogs, not company disclosures. Treat listing ranges as indicative; many listing pages carry no clear posting date.

## 1. What do e-commerce delivery riders earn in Hyderabad (fixed salary vs per-parcel, incentives, petrol)?

### Takeaway
Hyderabad e-commerce riders on payroll or vendor contracts are advertised at about ₹18,000-30,000/month gross. Most listings fall around ₹20,000-25,000, and the top of the advertised ranges (₹40,000-47,000) depends on high volume or incentives. Per-parcel contracts pay about ₹12-17 per successful delivery for e-commerce parcels (₹16+₹1 in one Hyderabad Meesho listing), and the rider usually pays for his own bike and fuel.

### Inferences
- **Base case for modelling.** One way to contract 3 riders is per-parcel at ₹14-17 per successful delivery, with the rider supplying bike and fuel. At 50 successful deliveries/day for 26 days, that is about ₹18,200-22,100 per rider per month, or about ₹55,000-66,000/month for 3 riders. The other way is a fixed salary of ₹18,000-22,000 plus a separate fuel allowance or per-km reimbursement. Expect fixed salaries to be quoted "with fuel included" by vendors at ₹22,000-28,000.
- **Benchmark against the DSP rate.** If a hub's own revenue per delivered parcel is about ₹15-25 (the Amazon-to-DSP figure above), paying riders ₹14-17 per parcel leaves a thin margin. Rider cost will be the dominant cost line.
- Listing upper ends (₹40,000-47,000) are recruitment marketing tied to very high volumes. Don't use them as the base case.

### Gaps
- No verified 2025-2026 Hyderabad per-parcel rate found for Ekart, Delhivery, Amazon, or Valmo specifically. The Meesho KPHB "16+1" listing is the only Hyderabad per-parcel data point.
- No AmbitionBox or Glassdoor Hyderabad pages were fetched with reliable sample sizes. Glassdoor's Shadowfax bike-delivery page has only 1 salary.
- No explicit petrol-allowance amounts (₹/km or ₹/month) found in any Hyderabad listing.

## 2. Realistic successful deliveries per rider per day; first-attempt success and RTO

### Takeaway
No authoritative Hyderabad productivity figure was found. Reported urban volumes are 50-100 parcels/day (80-100 in dense Delhi-NCR). Industry first-attempt success is about 72-84% blended (above 92% considered "healthy"), and RTO is about 20-35%, much higher for COD.

### Cited Findings
- Urban e-commerce delivery associates in Delhi-NCR: about 80-100 deliveries daily — [India.com, Aug 2025](https://www.india.com/business/delivery-boy-salary-how-much-do-amazon-flipkart-myntra-delivery-boys-make-each-successful-delivery-earns-them-rs-12-per-parcel-8031559/)
- Blog estimate of a "50 parcels a day" Flipkart rider (illustrative) — [PrivateJobWale](https://privatejobwale.in/flipkart-delivery-boy-salary-per-day/)
- Hyderabad Meesho listing covers a 3 km radius across 2 pincodes (500072, 500055), which suggests a dense beat — [WorkIndia](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/delivery_boy-kphb-hyderabad-5986942/)
- Shadowfax network scale: about 15 lakh+ orders/day and about 1.5 lakh monthly transacting delivery partners (Feb 2024). That averages roughly 10 orders per partner per day, but it includes part-timers and hyperlocal/food. An older Ken report said about 40,000 workers handled over 500,000 orders/day. — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/shadowfax-success-story/); [The Ken (old)](https://the-ken.com/story/too-many-cooks-in-shadowfaxs-hyperlocal-delivery-kitchen/)
- IFC case study: Shadowfax partners work about 6.5 hours/day on average and earn about $0.98/hour (2023, includes part-time) — [IFC](https://www.ifc.org/content/dam/ifc/doclink/2023/case-study-shadowfax-supporting-advancement-resilience-of-delivery-partners.pdf)
- Blended first-attempt delivery success rate in Indian e-commerce: 71.6% — [PointNXT 2026 benchmarks](https://pointnxt.com/blog/indian-ecommerce-logistics-benchmarks-statistics/)
- First-attempt success was as low as 73% in non-standardised-address areas and rose to 84% after Delhivery's address-intelligence engine (Kanpur, Patna, Bhopal) — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-last-mile-delivery-market)
- Above 92% first-attempt success is "healthy" for Indian e-commerce parcels — [ZenDMS](https://zendms.app/last-mile-delivery-cost-per-order-india/)
- India average RTO about 23%. COD failed-delivery rates in Tier 2/3 cities reach 40-49%. — [Amazon Shipping India](https://shipping.amazon.in/blog/last-mile-delivery-india-smb-guide)
- RTO 20-30% for prepaid and 30-40% for COD. COD is 60-65% of orders. — [Base.com 2026](https://base.com/en-IN/blog/india-ecommerce-benchmarks-2026-how-brands-reduce-rto-in-india-and-hit-accuracy-sla-targets/)
- COD RTO averaged 27% in 2025, versus 12% in metros — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-last-mile-delivery-market)
- COD RTO about 26% versus under 2% for prepaid (GoKwik data). India average RTO 25-35%. — [ShipSagar](https://shipsagar.com/ecommerce-logistics-india/)
- National COD RTO 24.8%, falling to 16.7% with automated NDR management — [PointNXT](https://pointnxt.com/blog/indian-ecommerce-logistics-benchmarks-statistics/)

### Inferences
- **Planning ranges for a 3-rider hub in Hyderabad.**

  | Area | Parcels attempted/day | Successful/day | First-attempt success |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Dense urban (Kukatpally, KPHB, Mehdipatnam-type pincodes) | 60-80 | 50-65 | about 80-85% |
  | Outskirts or semi-urban (Shamshabad, Medchal, Ghatkesar-type) | 35-50 | 28-40 | about 75-80% |

  The 12% metro COD RTO figure supports a higher success rate in core Hyderabad than the national average.
- **Base case.** About 50 successful deliveries per rider per day, so 3 riders deliver about 150/day or about 3,900/month (26 days). This is conservative but realistic for a new hub. 80-100/day is an upside case for mature dense beats.
- Failed attempts still use rider time and fuel. Under a per-successful-parcel pay model the rider bears this, which raises rider churn on poor beats.

### Gaps
- No Hyderabad-specific parcels/day or first-attempt success data from Delhivery or Shadowfax DRHPs or annual reports was retrieved. The Shadowfax DRHP text was not accessible in search results.
- RTO and first-attempt figures are mostly vendor and benchmark blogs with undisclosed methodology. Mordor and Amazon are somewhat stronger.

Petrol in Hyderabad is ₹116.15/litre (stable from late Oct to 6 Nov 2026). For a 60-80 km/day parcel route on a 100-125cc bike, fuel is roughly ₹4,000-6,000 per rider per month. EV rentals cost about ₹180/day (Zypp, Hyderabad) or ₹1,500-2,100/week (₹6,000-9,000/month), and many plans include battery swaps.

### Inferences
- **Petrol cost per rider.** Assume 60-80 km/day for a parcel beat and about 45 km/l for a commuter motorcycle (assumed figure, not sourced here). That is about 1.3-1.8 L/day, or ₹155-210/day. Over 26 days that is about ₹4,000-5,400 per month per rider, or about ₹12,000-16,000/month for 3 riders. Add about ₹1,000-1,500/month per rider for servicing, oil, and tyres, which is below ThePrint's food-delivery figure because parcel beats are shorter.
- **EV rental.** Zypp at ₹180/day for 26-30 days is about ₹4,700-5,400/month. Weekly plans of ₹1,500-2,100 are about ₹6,500-9,000/month, swaps often included. That is roughly equal to or above the petrol plus maintenance cost of a rider-owned bike. EV rental mainly helps riders without a bike, which widens the hiring pool. Bulky e-commerce loads may exceed a rental scooter's carrying capacity.

## 4. Statutory and compliance costs if riders are employees

### Takeaway
Telangana's minimum wage for Zone I (Hyderabad) is ₹16,000/month unskilled and ₹17,000/month semi-skilled from 1 June 2026, with VDA folded in. A rider on a ₹17,000-20,000 wage falls under ESI (₹21,000 ceiling; employer 3.25%) and the new ₹25,000 EPF wage ceiling (employer 12%) if the establishment is covered. Telangana's Gig Workers Bill (passed 30 March 2026) puts a 1-2% welfare levy on platform transactions, which falls on platforms and aggregators rather than a small hub.

### Cited Findings
**Minimum wages:**
- Telangana minimum wages (Shops and Establishments), revised from 1 June 2026, per month:

  | Category | Zone I (Hyderabad) | Zone II | Zone III |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Unskilled | ₹16,000 | ₹15,000 | ₹14,000 |
  | Semi-skilled | ₹17,000 | ₹16,000 | ₹15,000 |
  | Skilled | ₹18,500 | ₹17,500 | ₹16,500 |
  | Highly skilled | ₹20,000 | ₹19,000 | ₹18,000 |

  — [LabourLawHelp](https://labourlawhelp.com/telangana-minimum-wages/); [HRengage](https://hrengage.ai/compliance/minimum-wages/telangana)
- These rates are attributed to G.O.Ms.No.6 dated 30 May 2026, with VDA folded into the monthly figure and VDA accruing again from 1 Oct 2026. They use a CPI base of 443, a 26-day divisor for daily wages, and trainees at 75%. — [HRengage](https://hrengage.ai/compliance/minimum-wages/telangana); [Simpliance copy of the Govt abstract](https://www.simpliance.in/download/file/dXBsb2Fkcy9nb3Z0bm90aWZpY2F0aW9uL1RoZSBUZWxhbmdhbmEgTWluaW11bSBXYWdlcyAgTm90aWZpY2F0aW9uIChKdW5lMjAyNikucGRm)
- Earlier rates (Oct 2025-Mar 2026), for comparison: contract labour semi-skilled ₹15,058 and unskilled ₹12,745/month — [BoardHR](https://boardhr.blogspot.com/2025/10/telangana-government-revises-minimum.html). Shops semi-skilled Zone I ₹15,619.25 (Basic ₹6,500 + VDA ₹9,119.25) — [LabourCodes360](https://www.labourcodes360.com/minimum-wages/telangana)

**ESI:**
- Employer 3.25% and employee 0.75% of gross wages, total 4%, unchanged since July 2019. Wage ceiling ₹21,000/month (₹25,000 for persons with disability). Employees earning a daily average of ₹176 or less are exempt from the 0.75% share, but the employer still pays 3.25%. — [FactoHR](https://factohr.com/?p=49132); [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/esi-esic-contribution-rate-employer-employee-share/)
- The ESI Act was repealed from 21 Nov 2025 and folded into Chapter IV of the Code on Social Security, 2020. Existing rates and ceilings continue in the transition period, reportedly to 20 Nov 2026. — [FactoHR](https://factohr.com/?p=49132)

**EPF:**
- The EPF wage ceiling rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000/month after Cabinet approval on 16 Sept 2026, via notification S.O. 5109(E) dated 17 Sept 2026. Maximum contribution becomes ₹3,000 each (12% of ₹25,000). EPS pension computation may still use ₹15,000 until the scheme is amended. — [Corporate Professionals](https://www.corporateprofessionals.com/articles/epf-wage-ceiling-moves-to-%E2%82%B925000-key-changes-and-employer-impact/); [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/latest-updates/epfo-wage-ceiling-hike-union-cabinet-increases-mandatory-salary-limit-to-25-000-check-impact/article-200359/); [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/epfos-biggest-overhaul-in-70-years-what-changed-why-it-matters-and-what-comes-next/81689.html)
- Conflict: one outlet cites S.O. 2702(E) of 29 May 2026, which reportedly set ₹15,000 and is described elsewhere as superseded — [Legal Suvidha](https://legalsuvidha.com/latest-updates/epfo-raises-wage-ceiling-to-25-000-gazette-notification-issued-180926)

**Telangana gig workers law:**
- The Telangana Platform-Based Gig Workers (Registration, Social Security and Welfare) Bill, 2026 passed the Assembly on 30 March 2026. Its provisions:
  - a 1-2% levy on transactions funds a welfare fund for insurance, pension, and maternity benefits
  - platforms file quarterly electronic returns and must disclose pay and deductions
  - arbitrary algorithmic decisions are restricted
  - penalties are ₹50,000 for the first violation, ₹1 lakh for the second, ₹1.5 lakh for the third, and up to 5 times the arrears after that

  — [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/telangana/telangana-assembly-passes-comprehensive-gig-workers-welfare-bill/); [ANI](https://aninews.in/news/national/politics/telangana-assembly-passes-gig-workers-welfare-bill-202620260330180613/); [Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/telangana-assembly-passes-bill-for-welfare-social-security-of-gig-workers-1947300)
- Draft-stage details: aggregators register with the Board within 45 days of commencement. Aggregators with over 100 workers set up dispute committees. The fee must not be deducted from workers' base pay. — [PRS India draft bill](https://prsindia.org/bills/states/the-draft-telangana-gig-and-platform-workers-registration-social-security-and-welfare-bill-2025); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/columns/beyond-the-app-why-telanganas-new-law-is-a-major-step-for-gig-worker-dignity); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/telangana/aggregators-may-face-jail-term-over-failure-to-pay-gig-workers-welfare-fund-in-telangana-draft-bill-3494198)
- Conflict on who pays the levy: The Wire quoted an official describing it as collected from workers, while other sources place it on aggregators — [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/rights/telangana-set-to-become-third-state-to-introduce-legislation-for-gig-and-platform-workers)

### Inferences
- **Employee cost per rider.** Take a ₹17,000 semi-skilled Zone I wage, assuming ESI and EPF apply:
  - Employer ESI at 3.25% is ₹553.
  - Employer EPF at 12% depends on the PF wage. If the PF wage is the full ₹17,000 (no allowances split out), it is ₹2,040. If basic is set near ₹8,500-10,000 with the rest as allowances, it is about ₹1,000-1,200. The Labour Codes' 50%-wages rule limits how far this can go.

  So cost to employer is about ₹18,600-19,600 per rider per month before fuel, or about ₹56,000-59,000/month for 3 riders. Add fuel reimbursement (about ₹4,000-5,500 each) for a fully loaded cost of about ₹22,600-25,100 per rider, or about ₹68,000-75,000/month for 3.
- **Thresholds.** ESI and EPF registration are generally triggered by establishment size (commonly 10+ employees for ESI and 20+ for EPF). A 3-rider hub may fall below both, but this was not verified in this research.
- **Gig law exposure.** The levy targets platforms or aggregators, such as Ekart or Valmo. A small franchise or DSP hub engaging riders on contract is unlikely to be the levy payer, but may be asked to facilitate rider registration. Unconfirmed.
- **Minimum-wage floor on per-parcel pay.** If riders are employees, per-parcel pay must still meet the ₹16,000-17,000 floor. At ₹16/parcel that needs about 41-44 successful parcels/day over 26 days.

### Gaps
- No primary Telangana Labour Department G.O. text retrieved. The June 2026 rates come from compliance aggregators, and the category of employment applicable to couriers (shops and establishments vs transport vs courier services) was not confirmed.
- Not verified:
  - assent or notification status of the Telangana gig workers law
  - the final levy rate in rules
  - whether courier franchisees count as "aggregators"
- Accident or group personal-accident insurance premium cost per rider was not found. Rider health, accident, and life insurance via ESI is implied if covered.
- The employee-count thresholds for ESI and EPF were not verified.

## 5. Rider attrition, hiring difficulty, absence, and peak season

### Takeaway
No reliable Hyderabad or last-mile-specific attrition rate was found. Vendor estimates put city-level rider churn at about 18-24% (likely monthly or annual, undisclosed), against 16.2% overall India attrition across sectors in 2025. Festive peaks are a known capacity gap, but no Hyderabad temporary-rider rates were found.

### Cited Findings
- India overall attrition 16.2% in 2025, down from 17.7% in 2024 (Aon survey, all industries) — [Wisemonk](https://www.wisemonk.io/blogs/managing-attrition-in-india)
- Logistics vendor Edgistify claims 18-25% of last-mile spend is lost to driver attrition. It lists city churn rates of 18-24% (Mumbai 22%, Bangalore 18%, Guwahati 24%), methodology undisclosed. — [Edgistify](https://www.edgistify.com/resources/blogs/driver-retention-last-mile-churn)
- Mordor Intelligence lists "Driver and Rider Attrition, Compliance, and Peak-Hour Capacity Gaps" as a medium-term drag on the e-commerce last-mile market, strongest in metros during festive peaks — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/india-e-commerce-last-mile-delivery-market)
- Shadowfax has over 2 million registered partners but about 1.5 lakh monthly transacting (Feb 2024). The large registered-to-active gap indicates heavy churn and part-time participation. — [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/shadowfax-success-story/)
- Old (about 2016-17) Georgia Tech case notes cite a 2% attrition figure for Shadowfax. The text is fragmentary, so don't rely on it. — [GaTech](https://isye6339.scl.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/reports/shadowfax.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Turnover.** Plan for high turnover. Assume replacing 1-2 of 3 riders per year at minimum, more likely several. Each vacancy means lost delivery days, and the owner or a standby rider must cover.
- **Absence buffer.** Budget for one floating or part-time rider during festive peaks (Big Billion Days and Diwali, Sept-Nov). Volumes can multiply, typically 2-3 times or more, but no source for this was found.

### Gaps
